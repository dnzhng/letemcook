🇰🇷 Korean Gochujang Tofu Bowls

with Vegetables

Week of January 27, 2026

Servings: 10 (2 servings/day × 5 days)
Prep time: 20 minutes
Cook time: 35 minutes
Total time: 55 minutes

Overview

Bold, spicy-sweet Korean flavors meet crispy tofu and colorful vegetables in this satisfying wok-friendly meal. The gochujang (Korean red chili paste) creates a complex sauce that's spicy, savory, and slightly sweet - perfect for meal prep! This recipe minimizes cornstarch use (only ~3 tbsp total for sauce thickening) by pan-frying the tofu plain to achieve crispy edges.

Cost: $18-25 total (~$1.80-2.50 per serving)

Ingredients

For the Tofu:

  • 48 oz extra-firm tofu (3 blocks, 16 oz each)
  • ¼ cup neutral oil (vegetable, canola, or avocado) - for pan-frying

For the Vegetables:

  • 4 medium bell peppers (red, yellow, or orange), cut into 1-inch pieces (~5 cups)
  • 1.5 lbs mushrooms (cremini or shiitake), sliced (~6 cups)
  • 2 large heads broccoli, cut into florets (~6 cups)
  • 10 cloves garlic, minced
  • 2-inch piece fresh ginger, minced or grated

For the Gochujang Sauce:

  • ⅔ cup gochujang (Korean red chili paste) - adjust to taste
  • 10 tablespoons soy sauce (low sodium preferred, or tamari for GF)
  • ⅓ cup maple syrup or honey
  • 5 tablespoons rice vinegar (unseasoned)
  • 3 tablespoons toasted sesame oil
  • 3 tablespoons cornstarch - dissolved in ¼ cup water
  • ½ cup water (for thinning sauce as needed)

For Serving:

  • 4 cups uncooked jasmine or short-grain white rice (yields 10 cups cooked)
  • 2 bunches green onions, sliced (for garnish)
  • Sesame seeds (for garnish, optional)

Instructions

Step 1: Prep the Tofu (15 minutes)

  1. Remove tofu from packages and press to remove excess water. Place tofu blocks on a clean dish towel or paper towels, top with another towel, and place something heavy on top (like a cast iron pan or heavy books). Press for 10-15 minutes.
  2. Cut tofu into ¾-inch cubes. Pat dry again with paper towels if still moist.

Step 2: Make the Rice (While Tofu Presses)

  1. Cook rice according to package directions using 4 cups uncooked rice and the appropriate amount of water (typically 8 cups for white rice). This yields 10 cups cooked rice.
  2. Keep warm until ready to serve, or cool completely for meal prep containers.

Step 3: Prepare the Sauce (5 minutes)

  1. In a medium bowl, whisk together:
    • ⅔ cup gochujang
    • 10 tablespoons soy sauce
    • ⅓ cup maple syrup or honey
    • 5 tablespoons rice vinegar
    • 3 tablespoons toasted sesame oil
    • ½ cup water (add more if sauce is too thick)
  2. Taste and adjust - add more maple syrup for sweetness, more gochujang for heat, or more soy sauce for saltiness.
  3. In a small bowl, mix 3 tablespoons cornstarch with ¼ cup cold water until smooth. Set aside.

Step 4: Pan-Fry the Tofu (12-15 minutes)

Note: This is where we avoid using cornstarch on the tofu - we'll get it crispy through proper technique instead!

  1. Heat wok or large pan over medium-high heat. Add 2 tablespoons neutral oil.
  2. Add half the tofu cubes in a single layer (don't overcrowd!). Cook undisturbed for 4-5 minutes until golden brown on the bottom.
  3. Flip tofu pieces and cook another 4-5 minutes until golden and crispy on multiple sides. Transfer to a plate.
  4. Repeat with remaining 2 tablespoons oil and remaining tofu. Set all cooked tofu aside.

Step 5: Cook the Vegetables (8-10 minutes)

  1. In the same wok (wiped clean if needed), heat 1 tablespoon oil over high heat.
  2. Add mushrooms (6 cups sliced) in a single layer. Let cook undisturbed for 2-3 minutes until browned on one side. Stir and cook another 2-3 minutes until mushrooms are cooked through and most liquid has evaporated. Remove to the plate with tofu.
  3. Add bell peppers (5 cups) to the wok with another 1 tablespoon oil if needed. Stir-fry for 2-3 minutes until slightly softened but still crisp.
  4. Add broccoli florets (6 cups) and stir-fry for 2-3 minutes until bright green and slightly tender but still crisp.
  5. Add minced garlic (10 cloves) and minced ginger (2-inch piece). Stir-fry for 30-60 seconds until fragrant.

Step 6: Combine Everything (3-5 minutes)

  1. Return mushrooms and tofu to the wok with the vegetables.
  2. Pour the gochujang sauce over everything and stir gently to coat.
  3. Bring to a simmer over medium-high heat, stirring occasionally.
  4. Stir the cornstarch slurry (from Step 3) to recombine, then pour into the wok while stirring constantly.
  5. Cook for 1-2 minutes until sauce thickens and becomes glossy, coating everything beautifully. If sauce is too thick, add water 2 tablespoons at a time until desired consistency.
  6. Taste and adjust seasoning - add more soy sauce for saltiness, maple syrup for sweetness, or rice vinegar for tang.

Step 7: Serve or Meal Prep

For immediate serving:

  • Divide 10 cups cooked rice into bowls
  • Top with gochujang tofu and vegetables
  • Garnish with sliced green onions and sesame seeds

For meal prep:

  • Let everything cool completely (about 30 minutes)
  • Divide into 10 meal prep containers:
    • 1 cup cooked rice per container
    • Top with tofu and vegetable mixture
  • Garnish each with green onions and sesame seeds
  • Refrigerate for up to 5 days

Storage & Reheating

Storage: Store in airtight containers in the refrigerator for up to 5 days.

Reheating:

  • Microwave: Heat for 2-3 minutes, stirring halfway through. Add a splash of water if needed to loosen the sauce.
  • Stovetop: Heat in a pan over medium heat with a splash of water, stirring occasionally, until heated through (about 5 minutes).

Pro tip: The tofu won't be as crispy after refrigeration, but the flavors will be even better! For maximum crispiness, you can briefly pan-fry or air-fry the reheated portions.

Recipe Notes & Tips

Why This Recipe Minimizes Cornstarch:

Unlike traditional recipes that coat tofu in cornstarch before frying, this method achieves crispy tofu through high-heat pan-frying without coating. We only use 3 tablespoons cornstarch total (less than 1 teaspoon per serving) to thicken the sauce slightly. This keeps the recipe simpler and healthier while still delivering great texture!

Adjusting Spice Level:

  • Mild: Start with ½ cup gochujang and taste before adding more
  • Medium: Use ⅔ cup gochujang as written
  • Spicy: Use ¾-1 cup gochujang, or add gochugaru (Korean red pepper flakes)

Vegetable Substitutions:

  • Instead of bell peppers: Try snap peas, snow peas, or zucchini
  • Instead of broccoli: Try bok choy, cabbage, or asparagus
  • Instead of mushrooms: Use more broccoli and peppers
  • Avoid: Green beans, carrots, corn (as per your preferences)

Make-Ahead Tips:

  1. Press and cube tofu the night before, store in an airtight container in the fridge
  2. Prep vegetables (wash, chop) and store in containers
  3. Make the sauce up to 3 days ahead and refrigerate
  4. Cook rice ahead and refrigerate - it actually makes better fried dishes when using day-old rice!

Wok Cooking Tips:

  • High heat is your friend - the wok should be very hot for that characteristic "wok hei" (breath of the wok) flavor
  • Don't overcrowd - cook in batches if needed to maintain high heat
  • Keep ingredients moving - constant stirring prevents burning while ensuring even cooking
  • Have everything prepped before you start - wok cooking moves fast!

What Makes This Dish Special

Gochujang is a Korean fermented chili paste that's been made the same way for centuries. It's made from red chili flakes, fermented soybeans, glutinous rice, and salt. The fermentation process creates deep umami flavors with layers of spicy, sweet, and savory notes that you simply can't replicate with other hot sauces.

This dish is inspired by traditional Korean Jeyuk Bokkeum (spicy pork stir-fry) but adapted with tofu for a plant-based version. In Korea, it's considered comfort food and is often eaten with banchan (small side dishes) and kimchi.

The combination of crispy tofu, tender-crisp vegetables, and that addictive gochujang sauce over fluffy rice makes this the kind of meal you'll actually look forward to eating all week!
