Mapo Tofu Week

Tikka Masala · Japanese Curry · Mapo Tofu
Total Servings 10
Meals 3 dishes
Active Time ~80 min total
Equipment Single wok

Overview

Two quick "burn-down" meals (Tikka Masala + Japanese Curry) take care of 4 servings using sauces already in your fridge. The main event is Mapo Tofu (麻婆豆腐) — a Sichuan classic that's deeply garlic-and-chili-forward and burns through the rest of your leftover aromatics.

  • Chicken Tikka Masala over Basmati Rice — 2 servings (Monday)
  • Japanese Curry with Crispy Tofu over Rice — 2 servings (Tuesday)
  • Mapo Tofu over Rice — 6 servings (Wed–Fri)

All single-wok recipes. Total active cook time across all three meals: ~80 minutes.

🔪 Bulk Mise en Place

Do this all at once — each meal becomes mostly assembly. Plan for ~35 minutes of dedicated prep time.

Step 1: Press the tofu (start first — 30+ min hands-off)

  • 1 block extra-firm tofu (for Japanese curry) — press between paper towels with a weight on top
  • 3 blocks soft/medium-firm tofu (for mapo) — drain but don't press (they'll be blanched, not crisped)

Step 2: Cook all the rice at once (5 min active, 25 min hands-off)

Make all 5 cups in one batch — saves you cooking 3 separate batches.

  • 5 cups jasmine rice (covers all 3 meals — substitute 1 cup with basmati for tikka if you want, but jasmine works fine across the board)
  • 7.5 cups water
  • Pinch of salt

Rinse rice until water runs clear. Combine with water and salt in a large pot. Bring to boil, cover, reduce to lowest heat for 18 minutes. Rest 5 minutes covered. Fluff and portion into containers (2 servings each × 5).

Step 3: Prep all proteins (5 min)

  • 1 lb chicken thighs → cut into 1-inch chunks. Container labeled "tikka".
  • ¾ lb ground pork → keep in original package or transfer to container labeled "mapo".
  • 1 block pressed extra-firm tofu → cut into 1-inch cubes. Container labeled "curry tofu".
  • 3 blocks soft tofu → cut into 1-inch cubes. Blanch now: bring a pot of water to a simmer with 1 tsp salt, gently slide in tofu, simmer 2–3 minutes, drain carefully. Store in container labeled "mapo tofu".

Step 4: Toast & grind Sichuan peppercorns (3 min)

In a dry wok over medium-low heat, toast 2 tsp Sichuan peppercorns for 2–3 minutes, swirling. Grind in mortar/pestle or spice grinder. Store in small container — you'll use this twice (sprinkle during cooking + sprinkle when serving).

Step 5: Prep all aromatics (10 min)

Do this all at once with one cutting board session.

For Japanese Curry:

  • 1 yellow onion → sliced into half-moons. Container labeled "curry onion".

For Mapo Tofu:

  • 12 cloves garlic → finely minced.
  • 2-inch piece ginger → finely minced. Add to same container as garlic.
  • 6–10 Thai chilis → finely chopped. Add to same container as garlic + ginger.
  • 8 scallions → thinly slice, keep whites and greens in separate containers (they're added at different times).

Step 6: Pre-mix the mapo sauce (2 min)

In a jar or container, combine and shake:

  • 2 tbsp Shaoxing wine
  • 2 tbsp soy sauce
  • 1 tbsp dark soy sauce
  • 2 tsp sugar

Label "mapo liquid". Refrigerate.

Step 7: Pre-portion the mapo paste (1 min)

In a small container, combine:

  • 4 tbsp doubanjiang
  • 2 tbsp fermented black beans (chopped roughly), if using

Label "mapo paste". Refrigerate.

Mise en Place Output — your fridge should contain:

  • Cooked rice, portioned
  • Chicken chunks, raw
  • Ground pork, raw
  • Pressed extra-firm tofu cubes (for curry)
  • Blanched soft tofu cubes (for mapo)
  • Ground Sichuan peppercorns
  • Sliced onion (for curry)
  • Garlic + ginger + Thai chili mince (for mapo)
  • Scallion whites
  • Scallion greens
  • Mapo liquid jar
  • Mapo paste container

Meal 1: Chicken Tikka Masala

2 servings · ~15 min active (after bulk prep)

Step 1: Sear the chicken (5 min)

Heat 1 tbsp neutral oil in your wok over medium-high heat. Pat the chicken chunks dry, season with salt and pepper, and add to wok in a single layer. Don't crowd. Sear 3–4 minutes until golden, then flip and cook another 2 minutes. Chicken doesn't need to be fully cooked through — it'll finish in the sauce.

Step 2: Simmer in sauce (10 min)

Pour the 2 servings of tikka masala sauce directly into the wok over the chicken. Stir to coat. Bring to a gentle simmer, reduce heat to medium-low, and cook 8–10 minutes, stirring occasionally, until the chicken is cooked through and the sauce has thickened slightly.

Step 3: Plate

Reheat 2 servings of rice (microwave with a splash of water). Serve chicken over rice. Optional: top with fresh cilantro and a squeeze of lemon.

Meal 2: Japanese Curry with Crispy Tofu

2 servings · ~20 min active (after bulk prep)

Step 1: Crisp the tofu (8 min)

Heat 3 tbsp neutral oil in your wok over medium-high heat. Add the tofu cubes in a single layer and let them sit untouched for 3–4 minutes until the bottom is golden. Flip and crisp another side 2–3 minutes. Repeat once more so most sides are golden. Transfer to a plate.

Step 2: Sauté onion & build curry (10 min)

In the same wok with residual oil, add the sliced onion and a pinch of salt. Cook over medium heat 4–5 minutes until softened and starting to brown. Add the 2 servings of leftover Japanese curry and ¼ cup water (to loosen it back up). Bring to a simmer and cook 3–4 minutes until heated through and saucy.

Step 3: Combine & serve (1 min)

Return the crispy tofu to the wok and gently fold into the curry. Cook 1 minute to warm through. Reheat 2 servings of rice and serve curry over the top.

Meal 3: Mapo Tofu (麻婆豆腐)

6 servings · ~20 min active (after bulk prep)

About This Dish

Mapo tofu is the most iconic dish of Sichuan cuisine — silky tofu cubes braised in a deeply savory, spicy, numbing sauce built on doubanjiang, garlic, ginger, and Sichuan peppercorns. The Sichuan name describes five flavor dimensions: má (numbing), là (spicy), xiāng (aromatic), tàng (hot), and nèn (tender).

We skip cornstarch and reduce naturally — you lose a touch of glossiness but the flavor is identical.

Kept at stove: 2½ cups chicken or vegetable stock (low-sodium) · 4 tbsp neutral oil · 1 tbsp toasted sesame oil

Step 1: Brown the pork (5 min)

Heat 4 tbsp neutral oil in the wok over medium-high. Add the ground pork and break it up. Stir-fry 4–5 minutes until the water has evaporated and the pork is crispy and slightly browned. You want texture, not just cooked meat.

Step 2: Bloom the doubanjiang (2 min)

Reduce heat to medium. Push pork to one side. Add the mapo paste (doubanjiang + fermented black beans) to the empty side. Fry in the oil 1–2 minutes, stirring, until the oil turns deep red and the paste is intensely fragrant. This step is critical — don't rush it.

Step 3: Add aromatics (1 min)

Add the garlic + ginger + chili mince and scallion whites. Stir everything together. Cook 30–60 seconds until fragrant. Don't let the garlic burn.

Step 4: Build the sauce (2 min)

Pour in the mapo liquid jar and stir 30 seconds. Pour in 2½ cups stock. Bring to a simmer.

Step 5: Braise the tofu (10–12 min)

Gently slide the blanched tofu cubes into the wok. Don't stir vigorously — push the sauce around the tofu with the back of a spoon. Bring to a steady simmer and cook 10–12 minutes, gently swirling occasionally. The sauce reduces by about half and naturally thickens as the tofu absorbs flavor.

Step 6: Finish (1 min)

Sprinkle in 1½ tsp ground Sichuan peppercorns (reserve the rest for serving) and the scallion greens. Drizzle 1 tbsp toasted sesame oil over the top. One final gentle swirl. Kill the heat.

Step 7: Serve & store

Plate over rice. Sprinkle remaining Sichuan peppercorn over the top. For meal prep, portion rice and mapo into containers — the tofu continues absorbing sauce overnight and day 2 is excellent.

Reheating & Storage

  • Reheating: Microwave 2–3 minutes. Add a splash of water to the mapo before reheating to loosen the sauce.
  • All three meals improve overnight — flavors deepen across the board.
  • Mapo keeps 4–5 days. Sichuan peppercorn aroma fades slightly each day — sprinkle a fresh pinch when serving if you have extra.
  • Tofu texture: Medium-firm holds up to microwaving more robustly than silken. Handle reheated silken portions gently.

Tips for Mapo Tofu

  • Doubanjiang is non-negotiable. Look for "Pixian broad bean paste" (郫县豆瓣) at the Asian market. Lee Kum Kee works in a pinch. Without it, you're not making mapo tofu.
  • Blanch the tofu. The salt water simmer firms up silken/soft tofu and seasons it from the inside. Skip this and the cubes will crumble.
  • Bloom the doubanjiang properly. Frying it until the oil turns red transforms it from raw and harsh to deeply savory. Don't rush this step.
  • Pork should be crispy. Small amount, used as a flavor agent — get it dry and crisp before adding the doubanjiang.
  • Toast peppercorns whole, then grind. Pre-ground is flat. Fresh-toasted is night-and-day better.
  • Don't stir aggressively after tofu goes in. Use a gentle pushing motion with a spoon.

Flavor Profiles Across the Week

  • Tikka Masala: Creamy, warming, mildly spiced
  • Japanese Curry: Savory, slightly sweet, mellow
  • Mapo Tofu: Spicy, numbing (má), deeply savory, aromatic — the most intense of the three
