Pad Kee Mao

Thai Drunken Noodles with Tofu
Servings 10
Active Time ~30 min
Total Time ~50 min
Equipment Single wok

Overview

Pad Kee Mao — literally "drunkard's stir-fry" — is Thai street food at its most aggressive: wide rice noodles charred over screaming-hot heat, slicked in a savory-sweet sauce, and packed with garlic, Thai chili, and an absolute mountain of holy basil (or Thai basil). We're using up your leftover Thai chili from a few weeks back, and folding a little leftover lemongrass into the chili paste for an extra-aromatic twist.

Cooked in two batches of 5 — don't try to cram 10 servings in one wok or you'll steam instead of stir-fry and lose all the char.

Ingredients

Tofu:

  • 3 blocks extra-firm tofu (14–16 oz each, ~2.75 lbs total)
  • 3 tbsp neutral oil for searing
  • 2 tbsp soy sauce for marinating

Noodles:

  • 1.5 lbs dried wide rice noodles (or 2.5 lbs fresh if available)

Chili-Garlic-Lemongrass Paste:

  • 8–12 Thai bird's eye chilis (leftover supply — adjust to heat tolerance)
  • 1 red bell pepper, deseeded and roughly chopped
  • 16 garlic cloves, peeled
  • 2 stalks lemongrass, inner pale part only, thinly sliced (leftover use)

Stir-fry:

  • 6 tbsp neutral oil (split between two batches)
  • 2 red bell peppers, sliced into strips
  • 4 cups holy basil or Thai basil leaves, loosely packed (~4 large bunches)

Sauce:

  • 6 tbsp oyster sauce
  • 4 tbsp dark soy sauce
  • 3 tbsp regular soy sauce
  • 2 tbsp fish sauce
  • 3 tbsp brown sugar
  • 4 tbsp water

Finishing:

  • 2 limes, cut into wedges
  • Optional: chili flakes or chili oil at the table

Instructions

Step 1: Press and prep the tofu (15 min, mostly passive)

Drain all 3 blocks of tofu. Wrap each in paper towels or a clean kitchen towel and press under a heavy pan for at least 15 minutes (overnight in the fridge is ideal). Cut into 1-inch cubes. Toss with 2 tbsp soy sauce and set aside.

Step 2: Soak the noodles (15–20 min)

Fill a large bowl with hot tap water and soak the 1.5 lbs noodles for 15–20 minutes until pliable but still firm — they should bend without snapping but feel slightly underdone. Drain just before cooking. If using fresh noodles, microwave at 50% power for 1–2 minutes to soften, then gently separate the strands.

Step 3: Make the chili-garlic-lemongrass paste (5 min)

In a mortar and pestle (or mini food processor), pound together the 8–12 Thai chilis, 1 chopped red bell pepper, 16 garlic cloves, and 2 stalks sliced lemongrass until you have a rough paste. Don't over-process — you want texture, not a purée. Set aside.

Step 4: Mix the sauce (2 min)

In a bowl, whisk together all sauce ingredients until the sugar dissolves. Set aside.

Step 5: Sear the tofu (8 min)

Heat 3 tbsp neutral oil in the wok over medium-high heat until shimmering. Add tofu cubes in a single layer (work in batches if crowded). Sear undisturbed for 3–4 minutes per side until deeply golden. Transfer to a plate.

Step 6: Stir-fry — Batch 1 (5 min, have everything ready)

Wipe out the wok. Heat 3 tbsp oil over the highest heat your stove can produce until smoking.

  1. Add half the chili-garlic-lemongrass paste, stir-fry 20–30 seconds until intensely fragrant.
  2. Add 1 sliced red bell pepper, stir-fry 30 seconds.
  3. Add half the seared tofu, toss 30 seconds.
  4. Add half the drained noodles, toss with a scooping motion for 1 minute.
  5. Pour half the sauce over the noodles. Toss vigorously, then let noodles sit untouched 15–20 seconds to catch char. Toss again and repeat — this is where the wok hei comes from.
  6. Kill the heat, add 2 cups basil leaves, toss until just wilted (5 seconds).
  7. Transfer to meal prep containers.

Step 7: Stir-fry — Batch 2 (5 min)

Wipe out the wok briefly. Repeat Step 6 with the remaining half of everything: paste, bell pepper, tofu, noodles, sauce, basil.

Step 8: Pack and store

Divide between 10 meal prep containers. Tuck a lime wedge into each container. Refrigerate up to 5 days.

Reheating

  • Microwave: Sprinkle 1 tsp water over the noodles, cover loosely, microwave 90 seconds–2 minutes. Squeeze lime before eating.
  • Wok/pan (best): Heat 1 tsp oil over medium-high, dump in noodles, toss 2 minutes until heated through. The noodles will re-crisp slightly.

Notes & Tips

  • Heat tolerance: 8 Thai chilis = moderate-spicy. 12 = legitimately hot. Leftover chilis are likely riper/hotter than fresh.
  • Basil: Holy basil is traditional (peppery/clove-like). Thai basil (anise notes) is a great sub. Italian sweet basil works in a pinch.
  • Why two batches: A home wok can't maintain wok hei with more than ~5 servings at once. Two quick batches = great pad kee mao vs. sad steamed noodles.
  • Dark soy: Gives pad kee mao its signature deep brown color and slight molasses sweetness. If using only regular soy, add an extra tbsp of brown sugar.
  • Lemongrass riff: Not traditional, but using up leftover stalks in the chili paste adds a lovely citrus note. Skip if you'd rather stay strictly authentic.
