🇰🇷 Kimchi Jjim (김치찜)

Braised Kimchi with Pork Belly & Tofu

Week of February 23, 2026

Servings: 10
Prep time: 15 minutes
Cook time: 50 minutes
Total time: ~65 minutes

Overview

This is a braised kimchi dish (jjim style) rather than a soupy stew — the kimchi cooks down into a thick, concentrated, saucy glaze that clings to tender pork belly and tofu. Perfect for scooping into ssam leaves with ssamjang and rice for wrapped bites.

Your remaining leftovers (bulgogi, ssam vegetables, ssamjang, and banchans) serve as ready-made side dishes alongside.

Ingredients

Main Ingredients:

  • 1.5 lbs pork belly (leftover), sliced thin
  • 2.5 lbs napa cabbage kimchi (~40 oz), roughly chopped
  • ½ cup kimchi brine/juice (from the jar)
  • 3 blocks firm tofu (14-16 oz each), sliced into ½" rectangles
  • King oyster mushrooms (leftover), sliced into ½" rounds

Aromatics:

  • 2 medium yellow onions, sliced into half-moons
  • 2 bunches green onions, whites chopped, greens sliced for garnish
  • 8-10 cloves garlic, minced

Seasonings:

  • 3 tbsp gochugaru (Korean red pepper flakes)
  • 3 tbsp gochujang (Korean red chili paste)
  • 3 tbsp soy sauce
  • 2 tbsp toasted sesame oil
  • 1 tbsp sugar
  • 2 cups water or anchovy stock

Rice:

  • 4 cups short-grain or jasmine rice (yields ~10 cups cooked)

Instructions

Step 1: Prep Everything (10 minutes)

  1. Pork belly (1.5 lbs): Slice into thin bite-size pieces (~¼" thick). Tip: 10 minutes in the freezer makes slicing easier.
  2. Kimchi (2.5 lbs): Roughly chop into 1-2" pieces. Reserve ½ cup kimchi brine.
  3. Tofu (3 blocks): Slice each block into ½" thick rectangles.
  4. King oyster mushrooms: Slice into ½" rounds.
  5. Onion (2 medium): Slice into thin half-moons.
  6. Green onions: Separate whites and greens. Chop whites, slice greens for garnish.
  7. Start your rice in the rice cooker.

Step 2: Render the Pork Belly (5 minutes)

  1. Heat wok over medium-high heat.
  2. Add 1.5 lbs sliced pork belly — no oil needed, the fat will render.
  3. Cook, stirring occasionally, until edges start to crisp, about 4-5 minutes.

Step 3: Build the Stew Base (10 minutes)

  1. Add to the wok: minced garlic, chopped green onion whites, and sliced onions. Stir for 1 minute.
  2. Add 2.5 lbs chopped kimchi and ½ cup kimchi brine.
  3. Cook for 8-10 minutes, letting the kimchi caramelize and soften with the pork fat. This extended caramelization is key!

Step 4: Add Liquids & Seasonings (2 minutes)

  1. Pour in: 2 cups water, 3 tbsp gochugaru, 3 tbsp gochujang, 3 tbsp soy sauce, 1 tbsp sugar.
  2. Stir well to dissolve the gochujang. Bring to a boil.

Step 5: Braise (25 minutes)

  1. Reduce heat to medium-low for a gentle simmer.
  2. Cover and cook for 25 minutes, stirring once or twice.
  3. The kimchi should become very soft and the liquid will reduce into a thick, saucy glaze.

Step 6: Add Tofu & Mushrooms (10 minutes)

  1. Gently slide in all sliced tofu and king oyster mushrooms.
  2. Nestle them into the braised kimchi, spooning sauce over the tofu.
  3. Cover and cook for 10 more minutes on medium-low.

Step 7: Finish & Serve

  1. Remove from heat. Drizzle 2 tbsp toasted sesame oil over the braise.
  2. Taste and adjust seasoning.
  3. Garnish with sliced green onion tops.
  4. To eat ssam-style: Take a ssam leaf, add rice, top with pork/tofu and braised kimchi, dab of ssamjang, and wrap!

Meal Prep & Storage

  • Store: Let cool, then portion into containers with rice. Refrigerate up to 5 days.
  • Reheat: Microwave 2-3 minutes or stovetop with a splash of water.
  • Gets better with time: Day 2-3 will have deeper, more developed flavors as the tofu absorbs more sauce.
  • Ssam tip: Keep ssam leaves in a damp paper towel in a separate container so they stay crisp.

Notes

  • The kimchi makes or breaks this dish. Older, more fermented, sourer kimchi = dramatically better braise.
  • Don't skip the extended kimchi caramelization (Step 3). Cooking for 8-10 minutes in rendered pork fat builds the deep flavor.
  • Tofu goes in last so it holds its shape but still absorbs enough sauce.
  • Consistency check: The final dish should coat the back of a spoon. If too liquidy, cook uncovered to reduce.
  • No cornstarch needed. The sauce thickens naturally as the kimchi breaks down.
