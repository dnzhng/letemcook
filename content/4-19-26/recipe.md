# Kimchi Jjim (Braised Kimchi with Pork Belly & Tofu)
## Week of April 19, 2026

**Servings:** 10 (2/day × 5 days)
**Active Time:** ~40 minutes
**Total Time:** ~65 minutes
**Equipment:** Single wok with lid

---

## Overview

Kimchi jjim is the braised, thicker cousin of kimchi jjigae (soup). Rather than a soupy stew, this is cooked down to a rich, concentrated sauce that glazes the pork belly and tofu. Old, fermented kimchi makes the best jjim. Served ssam-style that week: rice + braised kimchi/pork/tofu + ssamjang in ssam leaves, with bulgogi and banchans on the side. Adapted from Korean home cooking tradition.

---

## Ingredients (10 servings)

**Protein & Grain**
- 1.5 lbs pork belly, cut into 1-inch chunks (leftover)
- 3 blocks firm tofu (14–16 oz each), sliced into thick rectangles
- 3.5 cups dry jasmine rice

**Produce & Kimchi**
- Large amount of kimchi (the older/more fermented the better) — about 3–4 cups
- 2 medium yellow onions, sliced into half-moons
- 2 bunches green onions, sliced (whites and greens separated)
- 1 bulb garlic, minced
- King oyster mushrooms — as much as available, sliced

**Sauce**
- 3 tbsp gochugaru (Korean red pepper flakes)
- 3 tbsp soy sauce
- 1 tbsp sesame oil
- 2 cups water (NOT 8 cups — this is jjim, not jjigae)
- Optional: 1 tbsp doenjang (Korean soybean paste) for depth

**For serving (on the side, not cooked)**
- Ssam leaves (perilla or lettuce)
- Ssamjang
- Bulgogi
- Banchans

---

## Mise en Place

- **Slice pork belly** into 1-inch chunks.
- **Slice tofu** into thick rectangles (~¾-inch). Do not press — you want it to absorb liquid.
- **Drain kimchi** slightly but keep juice — add the juice to the braising liquid for extra flavor.
- **Slice onions** into half-moons.
- **Slice green onions** — whites and greens separate.
- **Mince garlic** and set aside.
- **Slice mushrooms** and set aside.
- **Measure gochugaru, soy sauce, sesame oil** — combine in a small bowl.
- **Cook rice** per package. Fluff and set aside.

---

## Steps

### Step 1: Cook rice
Cook rice per package. Fluff and set aside.

### Step 2: Render and brown pork belly (8–10 min)
Heat wok over medium-high. Add pork belly chunks — no oil. Render and brown on all sides, 8–10 min. Leave fat in wok.

### Step 3: Caramelize kimchi (8–10 min)
Add kimchi directly to the pork fat. Stir-fry over medium-high heat 8–10 min — longer than you think. The kimchi should darken, reduce, and caramelize in the pork fat. This step is what makes jjim taste so much more complex than regular jjigae. Add garlic and green onion whites in the last 2 min.

### Step 4: Add onions and mushrooms (3 min)
Add sliced onions and mushrooms. Stir-fry 2–3 min.

### Step 5: Add liquid and seasonings (2 min)
Add 2 cups water (plus reserved kimchi juice if available). Add gochugaru + soy sauce. Stir to combine.

### Step 6: Add tofu and braise (25 min)
Gently slide tofu slices in. Do not stir vigorously — push sauce around the tofu with the back of a spoon. Bring to a steady simmer. Cover and braise 25 min, gently swirling occasionally.

### Step 7: Reduce and finish (5 min)
Remove lid. Increase heat slightly. Cook uncovered 5 more min until sauce reduces to a thick, glossy consistency that coats the back of a spoon. Drizzle sesame oil over. Add green onion greens.

### Step 8: Portion
Serve over rice. Accompany with ssam leaves, ssamjang, bulgogi, and banchans for ssam-style eating.

---

## Meal Prep Notes

- Stores up to 4 days refrigerated.
- Flavors deepen significantly overnight.
- Reheat gently with a splash of water.
- Ssam components (leaves, ssamjang, bulgogi, banchans) stored separately.

---

## Note on Jjim vs Jjigae

Classic kimchi jjigae uses 6–8 cups of water and is served as a soup. This is jjim-style: only 2 cups of liquid, extended caramelization, uncovered finish. The result is saucier and more concentrated — better for meal prep containers and for wrapping in ssam.
