# Pad See Ew Fried Rice with Tofu, Egg & Broccoli 🇹🇭

## Week of June 22, 2026

**Servings:** 10 (2/day × 5 days)
**Active Time:** ~30 minutes
**Total Time:** ~65 minutes (includes 30 min hands-off tofu press + rice cook time)
**Equipment:** Single large wok + rice cooker or pot

-----

## Overview

All the dark, sweet-savory magic of Pad See Ew — charred edges, dark soy glaze, scrambled egg, crispy tofu, broccoli, and bell peppers — but over rice instead of noodles. The technique is essentially the same: get the wok screaming hot, let things char and caramelize, and toss with the dark soy sauce. Rice actually picks up the glaze beautifully, and day-old rice (or rice cooled and slightly dried) gives you better texture than fresh.

**Cuisine logic:** Same Pad See Ew flavor profile — you've done Pad Krapow and Thai peanut, but never this particular sauce combination. The dark soy + oyster sauce + vinegar is a different flavor universe from your recent Indonesian and Jamaican weeks.

**Leftover usage:** Bell peppers (red/yellow/orange), broccoli, and eggs are all core to this dish.

**Adapted from:** [Hot Thai Kitchen](https://hot-thai-kitchen.com/pad-see-ew-new/), [Feasting at Home](https://www.feastingathome.com/pad-see-ew/), [Delicious Detour](https://deliciousdetour.com/pad-see-ew-tofu/), and [Thai Caliente](https://thaicaliente.com/pad-see-ewe-with-shrimp-and-tofu/) — converted from noodles to rice, scaled to 10 servings, adapted for regular broccoli and bell peppers.

-----

## Mise en Place

Prep everything before the wok gets hot. This moves fast once cooking starts.

**Rice (start first):**

- 5 cups dry jasmine rice, cooked and spread on a sheet pan to cool and dry slightly (ideally cook the night before or at least 1 hour ahead — drier rice chars better)

**Bowl 1 — Sauce (mix together):**

- 1/2 cup dark soy sauce
- 1/4 cup light soy sauce (or regular soy sauce)
- 3 tbsp oyster sauce (or vegetarian oyster sauce)
- 3 tbsp rice vinegar
- 3 tbsp sugar (white or brown)
- 1 tsp white pepper

**Bowl 2 — Eggs:**

- 8 large eggs, beaten

**Tray 1 — Tofu:**

- 3 blocks extra-firm tofu (14–16 oz each, ~2.75 lbs total), pressed 30 min, cut into 3/4-inch cubes, patted very dry

**Tray 2 — Vegetables:**

- 2 large heads broccoli, cut into small florets, stems peeled and sliced thin (~6 cups)
- 3 bell peppers (your red/yellow/orange leftovers), cut into thin strips
- 10 cloves garlic, minced

**Garnish station:**

- 1 bunch scallions, sliced
- White pepper
- Sriracha or chili flakes (optional)

-----

## Instructions

You'll cook the rice in batches in the wok — this is critical. Crowded rice steams; spread-out rice chars.

### Step 1: Cook the rice (30 min, mostly hands-off)

Cook **5 cups dry jasmine rice** in a rice cooker or pot. Once done, spread on a sheet pan or large plate and let cool at least 30 minutes. For best results, refrigerate uncovered overnight — cold, dry rice fries dramatically better.

### Step 2: Press the tofu (30 min, hands-off — overlap with rice cooling)

Wrap the **3 blocks of tofu** in paper towels or a clean kitchen towel. Place on a plate with a heavy skillet or pot on top. Let sit 30 minutes. This is your window to prep everything above.

### Step 3: Sear the tofu (8–10 min)

Heat **3 tbsp neutral oil** in the wok over high heat until shimmering. Add **half the tofu cubes** in a single layer. Don't touch them for 2 minutes — let a golden crust form. Flip and sear another 2 minutes. Remove to a plate. Repeat with **1 tbsp oil** and the **remaining tofu**. Sprinkle all seared tofu lightly with salt. Set aside.

### Step 4: Cook the vegetables (4–5 min)

In the same wok, add **1 tbsp oil**. Add **broccoli florets and stems** and **bell pepper strips**. Stir-fry over high heat for 2 minutes, then add **2 tbsp water** and cover for 1–2 minutes until broccoli is bright green and just tender-crisp. Remove to the plate with the tofu.

### Step 5: Scramble the eggs (1–2 min)

Add **1 tbsp oil** to the wok. Pour in the **8 beaten eggs**. Let them set for 15–20 seconds on the bottom, then break into large curds with a spatula. Cook just until barely set — they'll finish in the final toss. Remove and add to the tofu/veggie plate.

### Step 6: Char the rice (8–10 min, work in 2 batches)

This is the step that makes it. Wipe the wok if needed and add **2 tbsp oil** over high heat until smoking. Add **half the minced garlic (5 cloves)** and fry 15 seconds until fragrant. Add **half the cooled rice** and press it flat against the wok. **Don't touch it for 60–90 seconds** — let the bottom layer get dark and crispy. Then toss/flip and press flat again for another 60 seconds. Pour **half the sauce** over the rice and toss vigorously until every grain is coated and dark. Transfer to a large bowl.

Repeat with **1 tbsp oil**, the **remaining garlic**, **remaining rice**, and **remaining sauce**.

### Step 7: Combine everything (1–2 min)

Return all rice to the wok (or combine in a very large bowl). Add back the **tofu, vegetables, and egg curds**. Toss gently but thoroughly over medium heat until everything is warm and coated. Kill the heat.

### Step 8: Garnish and portion

Top with **sliced scallions** and a dusting of **white pepper**. Portion into 10 meal prep containers.

## Meal Prep & Storage

- **Fridge:** Up to 5 days in airtight containers
- **Reheat:** Microwave 2–3 minutes. Add a tiny splash of water if needed
- **Pro tip:** The dark soy glaze keeps the rice from drying out during storage. Day 2–3 are excellent — the flavors meld and deepen

## Tips

- **Dry rice is everything.** If you're cooking rice same-day, spread it on a sheet pan and refrigerate uncovered for at least 1 hour. Wet rice will steam and turn mushy instead of charring.
- **High heat, no stirring.** Press the rice flat and let it sit against the screaming hot wok. The char is the whole point — it's what separates this from regular fried rice.
- **Dark soy sauce ≠ regular soy sauce.** Dark soy is thicker, sweeter, and less salty. It's what gives Pad See Ew its signature dark color. If you still have kecap manis from last week's Indonesian dish, you can use it in a pinch (reduce the sugar by 1 tbsp since kecap manis is sweeter).
- **Batch cooking matters.** Overcrowding the wok drops the temperature and steams instead of searing. Two batches of rice is non-negotiable for good char.
